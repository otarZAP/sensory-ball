Why the XIAO ESP32S3 over a full ESP32 dev board: the XIAO is 21 × 17.5 mm and has onboard LiPo charge circuitry on the BAT+/BAT− pads. Plug a USB-C cable into the XIAO and it charges the cell — no separate TP4056 module needed. That deletes ~30 mm of board real estate from the stack inside the sphere.

RGB LED orientation (4-pin diffused)

Looking at the LED from above with leads pointing down, the standard pinout for the common-cathode 4-pin RGB LED in most resistor/component kits is:

PositionPinLength
1 (leftmost)Red anodeshort
2Common cathode (−)longest
3Green anodemedium
4 (rightmost)Blue anodeshort
Verify polarity before powering up. If your LED has a longest leg at position 2 and lights up red when that leg goes to GND and pin 1 goes to 3.3V through a resistor, you have a common cathode and the sketch defaults are correct. If lighting requires position 2 → 3.3V instead, set COMMON_ANODE true in sensory_ball_v1.ino.

Power notes

  • The 450 mAh 1S LiPo is wired directly to the XIAO's BAT+ and BAT− pads on the underside of the board. The XIAO regulates 3.7 V down to 3.3 V for everything else internally.
  • Charging happens automatically whenever USB-C is connected to the XIAO. Plug it in to charge — no extra hardware needed.
  • For runtime estimate: at typical mixed-activity (~30 mA average — MPU idle + LED partial brightness during smacks), the cell lasts ~12–15 hours. With LED off at rest most of the time, expect longer.
  • Add an inline SPST slide switch between BAT+ and the XIAO pad if you want a hard off-switch on the base.
